Thymus × Calcium in NLST

Two aging axes read off the same baseline low-dose CT: immune aging (the published thymic-health score of Bernatz et al., Nature 2026) and vascular aging (our cardiac calcium burden). Do they predict lung cancer independently, or are both stand-ins for smoking?

NLST T0 screenscase-cohort 6,041 pts / 1,003 cancersfull CT arm 25,031 pts / 1,030 cancers5 experiments · 14 hypotheses

Bottom line

  1. Cardiac calcium is a general, independent lung-cancer risk marker: HR 1.14 per SD [1.08, 1.22] after age, sex and smoking. It is unmoved by thymic health or the radiologist's emphysema read, stable across follow-up, and threshold-like. It adds a small but reproducible +0.01 test C-index to every baseline we tried.
  2. The thymic-health score reproduces the paper exactly (unadjusted high vs low HR 0.649 vs published 0.64). After adjustment it protects only against non-adenocarcinoma: squamous + small cell HR 0.83 per SD, adenocarcinoma 1.00, interaction p = 0.019.
  3. The two axes are linked but complementary. Lower thymic health goes with more calcium (partial ρ −0.10) and much more pericardial fat (−0.32). They share about a quarter of the squamous/small-cell signal.

Finding 1 · E5, full NLST CT arm

Thymic health protects against squamous and small-cell cancer, not adenocarcinoma

Pooled over histologies, thymic health's adjusted HR is 0.91 per SD (p = 0.004). Split by histology, that average hides a clean dichotomy: squamous 0.80, small cell 0.89, other/NOS 0.86, adenocarcinoma 1.00 [0.91, 1.10]. The Lunn–McNeil competing-risks interaction is 0.835 [0.72, 0.97]. It is unchanged by adjusting for emphysema.

We stress-tested it before believing it (E4, locked protocol). The direction holds in all 7 disjoint subsets (train/val/test, men/women, current/former smokers). Body-size comparators (pericardial fat, heart volume) don't show it. It isn't driven by indolent BAC-type adenocarcinomas, and it is stronger for stage III–IV (0.91) than stage I–II (0.98) cancers.

Candidate mechanism: thymic output (the naïve T-cell repertoire) matters most for the most mutagenized, neoantigen-rich tumours, the smoking-signature histologies. That would also explain why the paper's lung-cancer mortality HR (0.52) is stronger than its incidence HR (0.64). The alternative is residual pack-years confounding. It predicts that smoking-dose markers would absorb most of the effect, but they absorb about 25%. Deciding between the two needs pack-years from CDAS. The finding came from an exploratory comparison (p = 0.009 against a Bonferroni threshold of 0.01), and every later check used the same cancers. It is robust, but it has not been independently replicated.

Finding 3 · held-out test split, n = 1,983, 149 cancers

Prediction: calcium adds, thymic health doesn't (pooled)

no imaging marker thymic health includes calcium

Calcium gives +0.010 over clinical and +0.010 [+0.000, +0.020] over clinical + emphysema. The same gain appeared in the earlier R5 run. Pooled thymic health adds nothing, but for squamous/small-cell-specific prediction it adds +0.013 (C 0.720 → 0.733, p = 0.056, 58 events), while for adenocarcinoma it subtracts 0.016. That's the same dichotomy as Finding 1, seen on held-out patients. The cohort is case-enriched, so rankings are valid but absolute risks are not.
