# The Association of Coronary Artery Calcium With Noncardiovascular Disease: The Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis

- **Authors:** Handy CE, Desai CS, Dardari ZA, Al-Mallah MH, Miedema MD, Ouyang P, Budoff MJ, Blumenthal RS, Nasir K, Blaha MJ
- **Year / venue:** 2016, *JACC: Cardiovascular Imaging* 9(5):568–576
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jcmg.2015.09.020 · PMID 26970999 · PMCID PMC4860157
- **How verified:** PubMed record and abstract.

## Key findings
- MESA, n = 6,814, median follow-up 10.2 years. Cox models accounted for the competing risk of fatal CHD.
- Adjustment was extensive: age, sex, race, SES, insurance, BMI, physical activity, diet, **tobacco use**, number of medications, blood pressure, lipids, relevant medications and diabetes.
- CAC >400 vs 0: **cancer HR 1.53 (1.18–1.99)**, CKD 1.70, pneumonia 1.97, **COPD 2.71 (1.60–4.57)**, hip fracture 4.29. No association with dementia or DVT/PE.
- CAC = 0 vs >0: cancer HR 0.76 (0.63–0.92). The authors frame people with CAC = 0 as "healthy agers" and CAC as a marker of **vascular ageing** in general, not just coronary disease.

## Relevance to our project
- This is where "CAC as a general biological-ageing marker" comes from. It supports reading our cardiac calcium signal as vascular or biological ageing, and it predicts that calcium should correlate with other ageing markers such as thymic involution.
- The associations with COPD and pneumonia hint that CAC tracks smoke-related lung injury, which points to confounding by lung damage (emphysema) as well as by smoking. Emphysema on the same LDCT could be a useful additional covariate.
