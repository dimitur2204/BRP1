# Health Consequences of Thymus Removal in Adults

- **Authors:** Kooshesh KA, Foy BH, Sykes DB, Gustafsson K, Scadden DT
- **Year / venue:** 2023, *New England Journal of Medicine* 389(5):406–417
- **DOI:** 10.1056/NEJMoa2302892 · PMID 37530823
- **How verified:** PubMed record and abstract.

## Key findings
- 1,420 adult thymectomy patients vs 6,021 controls who had similar cardiothoracic surgery. The primary matched cohort was 1,146 pairs.
- 5-year all-cause mortality **8.1% vs 2.8% (RR 2.9, 1.7–4.8)**. Cancer **7.4% vs 3.7% (RR 2.0, 1.3–3.2)**.
- Autoimmune disease RR 1.5 (1.02–2.2) after excluding patients with preoperative infection, cancer or autoimmunity.
- In a subgroup (22 vs 19, about 14 years after surgery), thymectomy patients had lower new T-cell production (CD4+ sjTREC 526 vs 1,451 per µg DNA; CD8+ 447 vs 1,466) and higher proinflammatory cytokines.

## Relevance to our project
- This is quasi-experimental evidence that the adult thymus matters for cancer risk and systemic inflammation. It is the biological prior behind the thymic-health → lung-cancer hypothesis.
- The inflammation finding offers a route by which thymic loss could also relate to atherosclerosis and calcification, but this paper did not measure cardiovascular endpoints or CAC.
