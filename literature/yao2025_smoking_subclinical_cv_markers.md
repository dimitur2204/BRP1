# Association Between Cigarette Smoking and Subclinical Markers of Cardiovascular Harm

- **Authors:** Yao Z, Tasdighi E, Dardari ZA, Jha KK, Osuji N, Rajan T, Boakye E, Rodriguez CJ, Matsushita K, Simonsick EM, Lima JAC, Widome R, Cohen DL, Appel LJ, Khera A, Hall ME, Judd SE, Cole SA, Vasan RS, Benjamin EJ, Lotufo PA, Benseñor IM, El Khoudary SR, Barinas-Mitchell E, Janssen I, Psaty BM, Eaton CB, LaMonte MJ, Cawthon PM, Orwoll ES, Irvin MR, Bhatnagar A, DeFilippis AP, El-Shahaway O, Blaha MJ
- **Year / venue:** 2025, *Journal of the American College of Cardiology* 85(10):1018–1034
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jacc.2024.12.032 · PMID 40074467
- **How verified:** PubMed record and abstract.

## Key findings
- Cross Cohort Collaboration: 182,364 participants from 22 cohorts. Smoking intensity, pack-years and time since quitting were related to markers of inflammation (hsCRP, IL-6, GlycA), thrombosis and subclinical atherosclerosis (CAC, carotid IMT and plaque, ABI).
- Clear **dose-response with pack-years** for every marker, roughly 1–9% higher per 10 pack-years. The slope persisted beyond 20 pack-years, though flatter.
- In current smokers the blood markers plateaued after about 20 cigarettes/day, while the **atherosclerosis markers kept rising**.
- In former smokers most markers returned to never-smoker levels by 30 years after quitting, **except CAC, which stayed about 19% higher even more than 30 years after cessation**.

## Relevance to our project
- This is the mechanistic basis for "calcium as a proxy for cumulative smoking": CAC records lifetime smoking dose and does not reset after quitting. In NLST (≥30 pack-years, many former smokers) calcium probably captures pack-years *and* duration better than the binary current/former variable we have.
- Inflammation markers (IL-6, CRP) respond to smoking and, per Bernatz 2026, also track low thymic health. That suggests inflammation as a common pathway linking smoking, thymic involution and calcification, and so a positive smoking-driven correlation between low thymic health and high calcium.
