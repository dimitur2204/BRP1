# AI Body Composition in Lung Cancer Screening: Added Value Beyond Lung Cancer Detection

- **Authors:** Xu K, Khan MS, Li TZ, Gao R, Terry JG, Huo Y, Lasko TA, Carr JJ, Maldonado F, Landman BA, Sandler KL
- **Year / venue:** 2023, *Radiology* 308(1):e222937
- **DOI:** 10.1148/radiol.222937 · PMID 37489991 · PMCID PMC10374937
- **How verified:** PubMed abstract and PMC full text (methods and results excerpts).

## Key findings
- NLST, n = 20,768 (865 lung cancers, 4,180 deaths). AI-derived skeletal muscle and subcutaneous fat area and attenuation at baseline LDCT.
- Adjustment covered age, BMI, **quantitative emphysema (%LAA < −950 HU)**, **AI-derived CAC severity (4 levels)**, diabetes, heart disease, hypertension, stroke and the other PLCOm2012 risk factors (including smoking).
- Body composition **improved prediction of lung-cancer death, CVD death and all-cause death but NOT lung-cancer incidence** (likelihood-ratio χ² 2.53, P = 0.11 in men; 1.73, P = 0.19 in women).
- In univariable analysis, higher muscle attenuation in men was linked to lower lung-cancer incidence (HR 0.81 per 7.6 HU). This did not survive full adjustment.

## Relevance to our project
- This is a direct warning: in NLST, opportunistic body-composition markers that predict *death* often **fail to add to lung-cancer *incidence*** once PLCOm2012 factors and CAC are adjusted for. The calcium incidence signal we found (without pack-years) must survive a similar full adjustment set.
- Emphysema and CAC were both covariates here, which is a template for the adjustment set in a paper-quality version of our analysis.
