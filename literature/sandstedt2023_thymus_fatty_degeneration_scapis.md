# Complete fatty degeneration of thymus associates with male sex, obesity and loss of circulating naïve CD8+ T cells in a Swedish middle-aged population

- **Authors:** Sandstedt M, Chung RWS, Skoglund C, Lundberg AK, Östgren CJ, Ernerudh J, Jonasson L
- **Year / venue:** 2023, *Immunity & Ageing* 20(1):45
- **DOI:** 10.1186/s12979-023-00371-7 · PMID 37653480 · PMCID PMC10470174
- **How verified:** PubMed abstract and Europe PMC full text.

## Key findings
- SCAPIS Leukocyte substudy (Linköping): n = 1,048 randomly invited adults aged 50–64 (49% female), thymus assessed on CT by attenuation, size and a 0–3 score.
- 59% had complete fatty degeneration, 25% predominantly fatty, 10% half/half and 6.6% solid.
- Independent determinants of complete fatty degeneration: **age, male sex, high BMI, abdominal obesity, low dietary fibre**. **Smoking status did not stay significant** in the multivariable model, although former smoking was associated in univariate analysis. The authors mention limited power.
- Fatty degeneration and low thymic attenuation were linked to fewer naïve CD8+ T cells, which in turn went with lower TREC levels (thymic output).

## Relevance to our project
- This gives an imaging-immunology link (fatty thymus → fewer naïve T cells and TRECs) that is independent of the Aerts group.
- After adjustment, smoking is a weaker determinant of thymic fat than obesity. **BMI and adiposity are major shared confounders**, because pericardial/epicardial fat, one of our cardiac features, is strongly BMI-driven. A thymus–pericardial-fat correlation is expected to be negative and largely BMI-mediated.
- SCAPIS also measured CAC, but this paper did not relate thymus to CAC, which is a gap we note.
