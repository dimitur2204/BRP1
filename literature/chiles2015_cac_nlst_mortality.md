# Association of Coronary Artery Calcification and Mortality in the National Lung Screening Trial: A Comparison of Three Scoring Methods

- **Authors:** Chiles C, Duan F, Gladish GW, Ravenel JG, Baginski SG, Snyder BS, DeMello S, Desjardins SS, Munden RF; NLST Study Team
- **Year / venue:** 2015, *Radiology* 276(1):82–90
- **DOI:** 10.1148/radiol.15142062 · PMID 25759972 · PMCID PMC5137795
- **How verified:** PubMed record and abstract.

## Key findings
- Case-cohort design within NLST: five cardiothoracic radiologists read 1,575 LDCTs (210 CHD deaths, 315 non-CHD deaths, 1,050 survivors). Weighted Cox models were used.
- CHD death by Agatston category (reference 0): 1–100 HR 1.27 (0.69–2.53), 101–1000 **HR 3.57 (2.14–7.48)**, >1000 **HR 6.63 (3.57–14.97)**.
- A simple visual score (none/mild/moderate/heavy) did as well as Agatston: heavy vs none HR 6.95 (3.73–15.67). The same held for all-cause mortality.
- Conclusion: non-gated LDCT in heavy smokers gives CAC that is prognostic for CHD and all-cause death.

## Relevance to our project
- This is the reference showing that CAC on NLST LDCT is measurable and meaningful despite no ECG gating, which supports our heart-mask calcium proxy. The study did not look at lung-cancer incidence, so our endpoint is not covered.
- The case-cohort weighting in this study is a model for correctly analysing our own event-enriched NLST subset.
