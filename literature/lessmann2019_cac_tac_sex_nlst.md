# Sex Differences in Coronary Artery and Thoracic Aorta Calcification and Their Association With Cardiovascular Mortality in Heavy Smokers

- **Authors:** Lessmann N, de Jong PA, Celeng C, Takx RAP, Viergever MA, van Ginneken B, Išgum I
- **Year / venue:** 2019, *JACC: Cardiovascular Imaging* 12(9):1808–1817
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jcmg.2018.10.026 · PMID 30660540
- **How verified:** PubMed record (full author list, volume/pages) and abstract.

## Key findings
- Nested case-control study of 5,718 NLST CT-arm participants with automatic CAC and thoracic-aorta calcium (TAC), resampled back to the full cohort.
- **CAC was far more common and heavier in men:** prevalence 81% vs 60%, median volume 104 vs 12 mm³. Women had CAC similar to men about 10 years younger.
- TAC was equally common in both sexes (92% vs 93%; median 388 vs 404 mm³).
- Both CAC and TAC were associated with CV and all-cause mortality. TAC, but not CAC, was associated with non-coronary CV death. TAC added to CAC for CHD death in men only.

## Relevance to our project
- Calcium burden in NLST is strongly sex-dependent, and sex also drives thymic health (women higher). Sex is therefore a shared confounder of the thymus–calcium correlation, so we need sex-stratified or sex-adjusted correlations.
- Our heart-mask proxy leaves out aortic-root calcium by design (TotalSegmentator labels it as aorta), but it does include valve and annular calcium. TAC and valve calcium behave differently from CAC, so a sensitivity analysis splitting calcium by location would be informative.
