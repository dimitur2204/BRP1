# Coronary Artery Calcium Score and Incident Lung Cancer in a Population-based Cohort: The Screening Perspective

- **Authors:** Borchardt B, Schramm S, Erbel R, Schlosser T, In der Schmitten J, Grönemeyer D, Seibel R, Jöckel KH
- **Year / venue:** 2025, *Radiology: Cardiothoracic Imaging* 7(2):e240156
- **DOI:** 10.1148/ryct.240156 · PMID 40272252
- **How verified:** PubMed record and abstract. The full text is paywalled (HTTP 403). Details beyond the abstract come from secondary summaries (a web-search snippet and a healthmanagement.org news article) and are marked UNVERIFIED.

## Key findings
- Heinz Nixdorf Recall study (Germany): n = 4,605 aged 45–75, cardiac electron-beam CT, median follow-up 15.2 years, **111 incident lung cancers**.
- log(CAC+1): **fully adjusted HR 1.21 (1.10–1.32)**. CAC ≥400 vs 0: adjusted **HR 4.31 (2.19–8.51)**.
- Discrimination of CAC alone was poor: AUC 0.63 overall, 0.56 in the screening-eligible and 0.61 in the ineligible. The authors conclude CAC is a risk marker but would not improve screening efficiency.
- UNVERIFIED (secondary sources): the fully adjusted model reportedly included smoking history and cumulative dose (pack-years, environmental tobacco smoke), SES and air pollution. The association reportedly persisted, though weaker, in never-smokers. The authors attribute it to shared risk factors (smoking) and biological ageing.

## Relevance to our project
- This is an independent replication of the MESA effect size: about 1.2 per log(CAC+1) after adjustment. Along with MESA it makes our HR/SD 1.32 look plausible, not an artefact.
- The poor stand-alone AUC and "no screening benefit" conclusion match our finding that calcium alone does worse than the clinical model (test C 0.605) and adds only about +0.011 ΔC. We should expect incremental rather than stand-alone value.
