# Automated CT biomarkers for opportunistic prediction of future cardiovascular events and mortality in an asymptomatic screening population: a retrospective cohort study

- **Authors:** Pickhardt PJ, Graffy PM, Zea R, Lee SJ, Liu J, Sandfort V, Summers RM
- **Year / venue:** 2020, *The Lancet Digital Health* 2(4):e192–e200
- **DOI:** 10.1016/S2589-7500(20)30025-X · PMID 32864598 · PMCID PMC7454161
- **How verified:** PubMed record and abstract.

## Key findings
- 9,223 asymptomatic adults who had abdominal CT for colorectal-cancer screening, median follow-up 8.8 years, 19.7% with a major CV event or death.
- Five automated biomarkers: aortic calcification, muscle density, visceral/subcutaneous fat ratio, liver density and vertebral BMD.
- 5-year AUROC for death: **aortic calcification 0.743**, muscle density 0.721, V/S fat 0.661, BMD 0.646, liver 0.619, versus Framingham Risk Score 0.688 and BMI 0.499.
- Top vs other quartiles for death: aortic calcium HR 4.53 (3.82–5.37). Combining aortic calcium, muscle and liver density gave a 2-year AUROC of 0.811.

## Relevance to our project
- This is the founding "opportunistic CT" paper. Vascular calcification is again the strongest single marker, and combining organ markers adds information, which is the multi-organ logic behind our thymus + heart study.
- Its endpoints are CV events and death, not cancer incidence. For cancer the expected gains are much smaller, as Xu 2023 showed.
