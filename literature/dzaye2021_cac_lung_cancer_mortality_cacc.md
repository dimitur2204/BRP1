# Coronary artery calcium is associated with long-term mortality from lung cancer: Results from the Coronary Artery Calcium Consortium

- **Authors:** Dzaye O, Berning P, Dardari ZA, Berman DS, Budoff MJ, Miedema MD, Nasir K, Rozanski A, Rumberger JA, Shaw LJ, Mortensen MB, Whelton SP, Blaha MJ
- **Year / venue:** 2021, *Atherosclerosis* 339:48–54
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.atherosclerosis.2021.10.007 · PMID 34756729 · PMCID PMC8678296
- **How verified:** PubMed abstract and PMC full text (methods on smoking data).

## Key findings
- CAC Consortium, n = 55,943 aged 44–84 without heart disease, mean follow-up 12.2 years: 1,088 cancer deaths, 231 of them lung cancer. Fine–Gray competing-risk models adjusted for CVD risk factors.
- Lung-cancer mortality per 1,000 person-years: 0.2 at CAC = 0 vs 0.8 at CAC ≥400. **SHR 1.7 (1.2–2.6)** for CAC ≥400 vs 0, driven by women (SHR 2.3, 1.1–4.8).
- The association was stronger in people with any smoking history: **SHR 2.2 (1.2–4.2)**, and in women smokers SHR 4.0 (1.4–11.5).
- Limitation: smoking was mostly recorded as current smoking (yes/no); current vs former status was available only in a subset of 27,243. No pack-years, so residual confounding by smoking dose is likely.

## Relevance to our project
- The association is concentrated in smokers, which fits either CAC as a smoking-dose proxy or CAC as a susceptibility marker. This paper cannot tell them apart, and that ambiguity is exactly what our study can address, particularly once pack-years is available.
- The sex differences recur in the calcium literature, so we should pre-specify a sex × calcium interaction test.
