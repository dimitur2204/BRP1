## Key findings
- **Cohorts:** NLST (n = 25,031, baseline T0 LDCT) and Framingham Heart Study CT (n = 2,581). The model was developed on a separate set of 5,674 thoracic CTs (public IDC/TCIA collections). FHS and NLST were locked test sets.
- **Pipeline:** (1) a U-Net segments the thymic bed ("anterior mediastinal fat") and gives a centre of mass; (2) a 3D ResNet-50 is pretrained with self-supervised SwAV on thymic patches; (3) a shallow classifier is fine-tuned on expert thymic-involution grades (the Araki 0–3 fat/soft-tissue protocol). The raw output is P(thymus not "fully fatty degenerated"). Preprocessing: resampled to 1 mm isotropic, HU clipped to [−1024, 3072], min-max scaled.
- **Percentiling:** the raw probability was rank-transformed *within each cohort* to 0–100. Categories are low ≤25, average 25–75, high >75 (population quartiles). Scans that fail automatic QC go into "low". The authors say thresholds are population-specific and "no universal cutoffs can be assumed", and that kernel/scanner variability was not explicitly addressed. The Zenodo CSV has `ID, Thymic_health_continuous, Thymic_health_categories` (0/1/2 = 6,257 / 12,515 / 6,259). I could not tell QC-failed scans apart in it (no ties at the minimum).
- **Lung-cancer incidence (NLST, 6-yr follow-up):** high vs low KM incidence 3.4% vs 5.3%, **HR 0.64 (0.53–0.76)**; average vs low HR 0.78 (0.67–0.90). These are unadjusted. The association survived sex- and 5-yr-age-stratified Cox models adjusted for **smoking status + pack-years**, but only at **type III P = 0.036** (the adjusted HRs are in Fig. 3b; I did not extract them). Split by smoking status it was not significant: current smokers P = 0.051, former P = 0.25. Lung-cancer mortality, high vs low: HR 0.52 (0.44–0.63), and this survived adjustment in both current and former smokers.
- **CV and all-cause outcomes:** NLST CVD mortality high vs low HR 0.37 (0.31–0.44), all-cause HR 0.49 (0.45–0.53). FHS CVD mortality HR 0.08 (0.02–0.34). FHS incident CVD HR 0.26 (0.16–0.40), attenuated after smoking adjustment (P = 0.051). Adjustment sets: sex + age strata + smoking status + pack-years (NLST), then BMI and prior diseases (asthma, COPD, diabetes, heart disease, etc.), then age as time scale and a "healthy" subcohort.
- **Smoking and lifestyle:** in FHS, smoking duration, cigarettes/packs per day and pack-years were each negatively associated with thymic health (sex/age-adjusted). BMI was negatively associated, and so were triglycerides, glucose and blood pressure. HDL was positive, alcohol showed nothing, and 16/68 Olink inflammatory proteins (IL-6, IL-18, OSM, CXCL10/11, VEGFA) were negative. Chronic CRP ≥3 mg/L was linked to lower thymic health (P = 0.0012).
- **Coronary calcium was not examined:** neither the main text nor the supplement mentions CAC, Agatston, epicardial fat or any cardiac imaging feature. Model weights are not public ("intellectual property"; available through academic collaboration). The thymic-bed segmenter weights are on Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.18355851.

## Relevance to our project
- This is the source of our NLST thymus variable. Their adjusted lung-cancer association rests on **pack-years**, which we lack. Our reproduction with only age/sex/smoking status will be *less* adjusted than theirs, so a thymus HR in our subset will probably look stronger than the adjusted one.
- The authors never tested thymus against coronary calcium or cardiac fat, so a thymus × cardiac-calcium analysis on the same NLST scans is new. They are the same group behind DeepCAC/DeepCAC2 (Foldyna, Lu, Aerts, Nürnberg).
- The percentiles are ranked over all 25,031 NLST participants. In our case-enriched subset the categories will not be 25/50/25, so we should use the continuous score (or re-rank within our subset) and state which we chose.
