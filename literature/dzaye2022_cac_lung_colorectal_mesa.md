# Coronary artery calcium is associated with increased risk for lung and colorectal cancer in men and women: the Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA)

- **Authors:** Dzaye O, Berning P, Dardari ZA, Mortensen MB, Marshall CH, Nasir K, Budoff MJ, Blumenthal RS, Whelton SP, Blaha MJ
- **Year / venue:** 2022, *European Heart Journal – Cardiovascular Imaging* 23(5):708–716 (online 2021)
- **DOI:** 10.1093/ehjci/jeab099 · PMID 34086883 · PMCID PMC9016360
- **How verified:** PubMed abstract and PMC full text (Tables 2–3 and adjustment footnotes read).

## Key findings
- MESA participants without CVD or cancer, n = 6,271, mean follow-up 12.9 years, 777 incident cancers. Fine–Gray models accounted for competing CVD death.
- All-cause cancer incidence per 1,000 person-years: 13.1 at CAC = 0 vs 35.8 at CAC ≥400.
- Lung + colorectal cancer, CAC ≥400 vs 0: SHR 2.2 (1.1–4.7) in men and 2.2 (1.0–4.6) in women. There was no significant association for sex-specific cancers (prostate, breast, ovarian, uterine).
- **Lung cancer alone (n = 115):** log(CAC+1) HR **1.27 (1.19–1.37) unadjusted → 1.22 (1.11–1.34) fully adjusted**. CAC ≥400 vs 0: 4.87 → 3.22 (1.61–6.46). The fully adjusted model **included pack-years of smoking**, plus age, sex, race, BMI, activity, income, education, insurance and diet.

## Relevance to our project
- This is the best direct evidence on our question: **adjusting for pack-years attenuated the CAC–lung cancer association only modestly** (log-HR per log-unit went from about 0.24 to 0.20, roughly 17% attenuation). So CAC carries lung-cancer information beyond self-reported cumulative smoking, at least in a mostly non-heavy-smoking population.
- Their effect size is close to ours (our test HR/SD 1.32 for density-weighted calcium, adjusted only for age/sex/smoking status). In NLST, where everyone has ≥30 pack-years, the variance in pack-years is compressed, so attenuation after pack-years could be even smaller. That needs testing.
