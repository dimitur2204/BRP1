## Key findings
- Uses the same deep-learning thymic-health system as the companion paper. Cohorts: Harvard-NSCLC (n = 1,218, ICI-treated), Harvard-PAN (n = 2,258, other cancers) and TRACERx (n = 464 with preoperative CT). Scores were percentile-ranked 0–100 within the population, with categories ≤25 / 25–75 / >75.
- NSCLC on immunotherapy: PFS high vs low **HR 0.65 (0.54–0.77)**, OS **HR 0.56 (0.46–0.68)**. Both held after adjustment for sex, age, ECOG, histology, PD-L1, TMB and treatment line, and within PD-L1 and TMB subgroups.
- Biological validation in TRACERx: high thymic health had higher blood sjTREC (a direct readout of thymic output; n = 45), higher TCR α/β diversity and higher tumour T-cell fraction. That supports the score as a proxy for thymic output, not just mediastinal fat.
- TRACERx disease-free survival high vs low HR 0.62 (0.43–0.89) unadjusted. It attenuated to 0.84 (0.57–1.24) after sex, age, stage and adjuvant therapy, which shows how much of the signal clinical covariates absorb.
- Associations of thymic health with age, sex and **smoking history** in the pooled Harvard cohorts are given only in Extended Data Fig. 9. I did not extract the coefficients. Cardiac features and coronary calcium were not studied.

## Relevance to our project
- This is the main evidence that the CT score reflects immune function (TRECs, TCR diversity). That justifies reading thymic health as "immune ageing" in our hypothesis, as opposed to vascular ageing.
- The attenuation seen in TRACERx after clinical adjustment is a warning: thymic-health effects can shrink a lot once confounders are controlled. We should expect smaller adjusted effects in NLST without pack-years.
