# Normal thymus in adults: appearance on CT and associations with age, sex, BMI and smoking

- **Authors:** Araki T, Nishino M, Gao W, Dupuis J, Hunninghake GM, Murakami T, Washko GR, O'Connor GT, Hatabu H
- **Year / venue:** 2016, *European Radiology* 26(1):15–24
- **DOI:** 10.1007/s00330-015-3796-y · PMID 25925358 · PMCID PMC4847950
- **How verified:** PubMed abstract and PMC full text.

## Key findings
- Framingham Heart Study CT participants, n = 2,540 (mean age 58.9, 51% female). A 4-point visual thymic score based on the fat-to-soft-tissue ratio. This is the protocol later used to fine-tune the Bernatz 2026 model.
- 74% had complete fatty replacement (score 0), 18% predominantly fatty, 7% half/half, 1% solid soft tissue. Inter- and intra-observer weighted κ 0.83 and 0.87.
- Women had higher scores (less fat) at ages 40–69 (P < 0.001).
- **Smoking:** after age/sex adjustment, participants with score 0 were more often **former smokers (49%)** and had **more pack-years** (mean 20.3, P = 0.04). Those with a solid thymus were more often never-smokers (64%). Higher BMI went with more fatty thymus (P < 0.001), also adjusted for pack-years.

## Relevance to our project
- Independent, pre-deep-learning evidence that **cumulative smoking dose (pack-years) goes with thymic involution**. So thymic health is at least partly a smoking-exposure marker, just like calcium.
- The effect of pack-years is modest (P = 0.04 in n = 2,540), much weaker than age and sex. We should expect only a small smoking-mediated part of any thymus–calcium correlation.
