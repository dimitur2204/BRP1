# CT-Based Thymic Morphology as an Imaging Surrogate of Immune Ageing and Its Association with Coronary Artery Calcification — A Hypothesis-Generating Observational Study

- **Authors:** Walther IL, Mostafa K, Horr A, Freitag-Wolf S, Seoudy H, Müller OJ, Krutmann S, Jansen O, Langguth P
- **Year / venue:** 2026, *Biomedicines* 14(4):883 (published 13 Apr 2026)
- **DOI:** 10.3390/biomedicines14040883 · PMID 42072424 · PMCID PMC13113726
- **How verified:** PubMed abstract and Europe PMC full text.

## Key findings
- Retrospective, single centre: n = 206 patients aged 40–64 (126 men) who had ECG-triggered, contrast-enhanced thoracic CT. Agatston CAC was measured on virtual non-contrast spectral reconstructions, and thymus was graded 0–4 by fatty replacement.
- Complete fatty thymus was more common in men (51/126 vs 18/80, P = 0.011).
- **Inverse thymus–CAC association:** linear regression B = −28.8 Agatston units per thymus grade (−45.3 to −12.3; P = 0.001) unadjusted, and **B = −22.2 (−41.7 to −2.6; P = 0.03) after age and sex**. No interaction with age or sex.
- There was **no adjustment for smoking, BMI or other CV risk factors** (the authors say this explicitly), and the sample was a clinical referral population.

## Relevance to our project
- This is the **only direct study of thymus vs coronary calcium I found**. It supports the hypothesis that immune and vascular ageing are correlated (preserved thymus goes with less CAC) beyond age and sex.
- It is small and unadjusted for smoking and BMI, so NLST (n > 20,000 with thymic health available) can test the same link with far more power and with smoking adjustment. That is a clear contribution.
- About 23% of the unadjusted slope (−28.8 → −22.2) was removed by age and sex alone. We should expect further attenuation after smoking and BMI.
