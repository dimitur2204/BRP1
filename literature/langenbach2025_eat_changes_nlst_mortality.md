# Association of Epicardial Adipose Tissue Changes on Serial Chest CT Scans with Mortality: Insights from the National Lung Screening Trial

- **Authors:** Langenbach IL, Hadzic I, Zeleznik R, Langenbach MC, Maintz D, Mayrhofer T, Lu MT, Aerts HJWL, Foldyna B
- **Year / venue:** 2025, *Radiology* 314(2):e240473
- **DOI:** 10.1148/radiol.240473 · PMID 39964263 · PMCID PMC11868846
- **How verified:** PubMed record and abstract.

## Key findings
- NLST, n = 20,661 with baseline and 2-year LDCT, median follow-up 10.4 years: 3,483 deaths (816 CV, **705 lung cancer**).
- On average EAT volume rose by 2.5 cm³/m² and density fell by 0.5 HU over 2 years. "Typical" change was −7% to +11% for volume and −3% to +2% for density.
- Atypical EAT volume increase (HR 1.15) and decrease (HR 1.34) predicted all-cause mortality. A **rise in EAT density predicted all-cause (HR 1.29), CV (HR 1.29) and lung-cancer mortality (HR 1.30, 1.07–1.57)**.
- Models were adjusted for baseline EAT, age, sex, race, **smoking, pack-years**, CV history, diabetes, education, BMI and **CAC**.

## Relevance to our project
- This is the only NLST evidence linking a cardiac fat feature to a **lung-cancer** outcome (mortality, not incidence). Rising fat density, which may signal cachexia or inflammation, points more to disease course than to baseline risk.
- The paper shows the standard NLST adjustment set used by this group (smoking, pack-years, BMI, CAC). Our cardiac features will be judged against that set, so we need pack-years and BMI.
